# Study Methodology, Practice Testing & Exam Thoughts

## Study Methodology

While practical work in the Azure Portal formed a large part of my preparation, it was only one component. I followed a relatively consistent structure:

**Per domain:**

1. High-level pass using Scott Duffy’s Udemy videos.
2. Read the official Microsoft Learn material.
3. Hands-on work in the Azure Portal for each point in the Microsoft AZ-104 syllabus.
4. Use Copilot and Gemini to generate and answer domain-specific exam-style questions (I found these two models the most useful due to Copilot’s Microsoft grounding and Gemini’s lack of rate limits). I always double-checked anything that seemed inaccurate or outdated against Microsoft Learn or a stronger model like Claude.

I was fortunate to be able to dedicate significant time to this process (approximately 4 weeks of essentially full-time hours). Full-time workers and those with other commitments will almost certainly need longer.

## Practice Testing

I began full-length practice tests around 10 days before the exam. The most important habit was thoroughly reviewing every question (correct and incorrect) after each test. This was rather time-consuming, but I would not have passed without doing so.

I also fed the results of every practice test into a single Claude conversation, which helped track progress per domain and identify weak areas over time.

**Practice Test Results:**

| Practice Test                    | Score    |
|----------------------------------|----------|
| Whizlabs part-length             | ~50%     |
| Microsoft Learn practice         | ~66%     |
| Whizlabs full test #1            | 63%      |
| Scott Duffy practice test        | 64%      |
| Whizlabs full test #2            | 80%      |
| Whizlabs full test #3            | 81%      |
| First MeasureUp attempt          | 35%      |
| Second MeasureUp attempt         | 64%      |

Of all the practice tests, MeasureUp #1 was the most valuable to me. It was far more difficult than any test I had done up until that point and I took some time post-test to analyse what happened fully. I worked out that my overall knowledge of Azure remained fairly sound but that my low score came primarily from a different style of questioning and from not finishing all the questions in time. I learned from that test that I had to approach the real exam differently, with time discipline having a higher priority. The dividends of this strategy began to show in the next attempt and ultimately in the real exam.

## Exam Thoughts

My exam consisted of 56 questions, including a case study with 4 questions at the end. I certainly found it challenging, primarily due to time pressure. I had to be ruthless with time management, often even scanning the potential answers first for context before fully reading the question/scenario.

My exam was heavily weighted towards Identities, Governance, Access Management, and Policy.

The exam is difficult but achievable if you put in the work. In my view, thorough review of practice tests (especially the harder ones like MeasureUp), targeted use of large language models, and hands-on portal work were the biggest factors in my success.
